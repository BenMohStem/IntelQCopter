1. What is T?

Each spinning propeller pushes air down. The air exerts an upward force on the propeller, motor and frame. Call that force's magnitude TiT_i. Four parallel rotors contribute to the same upward direction when the drone is level:

T=TFL+TFR+TRR+TRL.T=T_{FL}+T_{FR}+T_{RR}+T_{RL}.

FL means front-left, FR front-right, RR rear-right, RL rear-left. This is the logical order in the C mixer. It still needs checking against physical wiring.

Gravity supplies a downward force mgmg. Choose upward as positive. Newton's law says net force equals mass times acceleration:

T⏟upward−mg⏟downward=mh¨⏟mass times vertical acceleration.\underbrace{T}_{\text{upward}} -\underbrace{mg}_{\text{downward}} =\underbrace{m\ddot h}_{\text{mass times vertical acceleration}}.

Level hover means no vertical acceleration and no vertical speed. Substitute h¨=0\ddot h=0:

T0−mg=m(0)=0⟹T0=mg.T_0-mg=m(0)=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_0=mg.

With a centered center of mass and equal loading, each rotor supplies a quarter:

Ti0=mg4.T_{i0}=\frac{mg}{4}.

Example only: choose m=1.2m=1.2 kg and g=9.80665g=9.80665 m/s². Then

T0=1.2(9.80665)=11.76798 N,Ti0=2.941995 N.T_0=1.2(9.80665)=11.76798\ \mathrm N, \qquad T_{i0}=2.941995\ \mathrm N.

If the motors add 1.2 N of total thrust above this hover value, with no drag,

h¨=(11.76798+1.2)−11.767981.2=1 m/s2.\ddot h=\frac{(11.76798+1.2)-11.76798}{1.2} =1\ \mathrm{m/s^2}.

Notice that this is acceleration, not speed. Starting at rest, that acceleration would produce 1 m/s after one second if it remained constant.

2.1 Why force multiplied by distance is torque

A force at the center lifts the drone without that force's lever arm turning it. The same vertical force away from the center also turns it. The perpendicular distance to the roll axis is the motor's y coordinate. Therefore its roll torque is yiTiy_iT_i. The corresponding signed pitch torque is −xiTi-x_iT_i.

The vector form makes both signs explicit. Here the force has only a z component:

[xiyi0]×[00Ti]=[yiTi−0⋅00⋅0−xiTixi⋅0−yi⋅0]=[yiTi−xiTi0].\begin{bmatrix}x_i\\y_i\\0\end{bmatrix} \times \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\T_i\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}y_iT_i-0\cdot0\\0\cdot0-x_iT_i\\x_i\cdot0-y_i\cdot0\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}y_iT_i\\-x_iT_i\\0\end{bmatrix}.

Add the four rotor contributions:

τx=a(TFL−TFR−TRR+TRL),\tau_x=a(T_{FL}-T_{FR}-T_{RR}+T_{RL}),
τy=a(−TFL−TFR+TRR+TRL).\tau_y=a(-T_{FL}-T_{FR}+T_{RR}+T_{RL}).

Try a number: increase each left rotor by 0.10 N and decrease each right rotor by 0.10 N. The total thrust change is zero, but the roll torque is not:

δT=0.10−0.10−0.10+0.10=0,\delta T=0.10-0.10-0.10+0.10=0,
δτx=a(0.10+0.10+0.10+0.10)=0.159099(0.40)=0.0636396 N m.\delta\tau_x=a(0.10+0.10+0.10+0.10) =0.159099(0.40)=0.0636396\ \mathrm{N\,m}.

This is how we command a roll while approximately preserving altitude near hover.

2.2 Yaw comes from propeller reaction torque

Vertical thrust at a horizontal lever arm gives no yaw term in the cross product above. Yaw instead comes from the air's reaction to propeller rotation. Counter-rotating pairs allow those reactions to cancel at hover.

Use a local proportionality κ\kappa with units of meters, since torque/thrust has units (N m)/N(\mathrm{N,m})/\mathrm N. Choose the reaction signs used by our logical mixer:

τz=κ(−TFL+TFR−TRR+TRL).\tau_z=\kappa(-T_{FL}+T_{FR}-T_{RR}+T_{RL}).

The negative/positive signs specify torque on the aircraft. They are a provisional spin convention, not a verified propeller installation. Changing spin order changes this row.

3. Specify the operating point and the unknowns

An operating point is the condition around which we calculate small changes. Ours is level, stationary hover, with zero yaw angle used as the world-axis reference:

ϕ0=θ0=ψ0=0,vx0=vy0=vz0=0,p0=q0=r0=0,T0=mg.\phi_0=\theta_0=\psi_0=0,\quad v_{x0}=v_{y0}=v_{z0}=0,\quad p_0=q_0=r_0=0,\quad T_0=mg.

Define a perturbation as the departure from that condition:

δT=T−T0,δui=ui−ui0,δh=h−h0.\delta T=T-T_0,\quad \delta u_i=u_i-u_{i0},\quad \delta h=h-h_0.

If a motor supplies 3.041995 N while its hover value is 2.941995 N, its perturbation is 0.10 N. The symbol δ\delta does not mean uncertainty here; it means a change from the chosen equilibrium.

3.1 How the F450 geometry contributes to inertia

For roll, a point mass at (xi,yi,zi)(x_i,y_i,z_i) contributes mi(yi2+zi2)m_i(y_i^2+z_i^2). Moving the mass farther from the roll axis increases the resistance to roll acceleration. Four identical motor/ESC groups of mass mem_e at (±a,±a,0)(\pm a,\pm a,0) contribute

This is the parallel-axis theorem. The frame has distributed mass and must also be included. We cannot infer all inertia values from 150 g of estimated battery mass alone.

4. Build the linear differential equations

4.1 Height: remove the constant hover force

Start with upward thrust minus weight minus vertical drag:

mh¨=T−mg−dzh˙.m\ddot h=T-mg-d_z\dot h.

Insert T=mg+δTT=mg+\delta T and h=h0+δhh=h_0+\delta h. The reference h0h_0 is constant, so its derivatives are zero:

mδh¨=(mg+δT)−mg−dzδh˙.m\delta\ddot h=(mg+\delta T)-mg-d_z\delta\dot h.

Cancel the two mgmg terms and put the drag term on the left:

mδh¨+dzδh˙=δT.\boxed{m\delta\ddot h+d_z\delta\dot h=\delta T.}

This is second order because height's second derivative appears. It is linear because the coefficients m,dzm,d_z are constants and no unknown states multiply each other. Gravity has not disappeared physically: it is balanced by the constant hover thrust.

4.2 Roll and pitch: the rotational version of Newton's law

For small rotations near level hover and approximately principal body axes, torque equals inertia times angular acceleration:

Jxp˙=τx,Jyq˙=τy,Jzr˙=τz.J_x\dot p=\tau_x,\qquad J_y\dot q=\tau_y,\qquad J_z\dot r=\tau_z.

Near the operating point, body rates coincide with Euler-angle derivatives:

ϕ˙=p,θ˙=q,ψ˙=r.\dot\phi=p,\quad\dot\theta=q,\quad\dot\psi=r.

Substitute the first equality into the roll equation and repeat for pitch:

Jxϕ¨=τx,Jyθ¨=τy.\boxed{J_x\ddot\phi=\tau_x,\qquad J_y\ddot\theta=\tau_y.}

The approximation omits products of angular rates. At small rates their effect is of higher order than the terms retained here. It is the reason these equations are a local hover model. We do not need to carry those omitted terms through this tutorial.

Using the torque example from §2, with assumed Jx=0.012J_x=0.012:

ϕ¨=0.06363960.012=5.3033 rad/s2.\ddot\phi=\frac{0.0636396}{0.012}=5.3033\ \mathrm{rad/s^2}.

The yaw controller regulates rr, not ψ\psi. Its plant therefore needs only one integration from yaw torque to yaw rate, plus the motor dynamics added below.

4.3 Horizontal motion: the multivariable connection

For small tilt around our zero-heading reference, pitch directs thrust along +x, and positive roll directs it along −y. A small angle in radians gives approximately that same fraction of hover thrust sideways:

Fx≈mgθ,Fy≈−mgϕ.F_x\approx mg\theta,\qquad F_y\approx-mg\phi.

Apply Newton's law and divide every term by mm:

mx¨=mgθ−dxx˙⟹x¨=gθ−dxmx˙,m\ddot x=mg\theta-d_x\dot x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\ddot x=g\theta-\frac{d_x}{m}\dot x,}
y¨=−gϕ−dymy˙.\boxed{\ddot y=-g\phi-\frac{d_y}{m}\dot y.}

For a 5° pitch, convert to radians before using this physical equation:

θ=5π180=0.0872665,x¨≈9.80665(0.0872665)=0.8558 m/s2\theta=5\frac{\pi}{180}=0.0872665, \qquad \ddot x\approx9.80665(0.0872665)=0.8558\ \mathrm{m/s^2}

at zero horizontal speed. This explains why an autonomous position controller can request pitch/roll angles from the flight controller. The current six loops do not themselves close horizontal position feedback.

4.4 Keep motor response delay while staying linear

Let δTi\delta T_i be a rotor's actual force change. A command does not create that force instantly. A first-order local motor/propeller model is

τmδT˙i+δTi=kTδui.\boxed{\tau_m\delta\dot T_i+\delta T_i=k_T\delta u_i.}

Rearrange it as an update direction:

δT˙i=kTδui−δTiτm.\delta\dot T_i=\frac{k_T\delta u_i-\delta T_i}{\tau_m}.

If the desired force change is 0.6 N and the current force change is zero, the initial slope is 0.6/0.04=150.6/0.04=15 N/s. As actual thrust approaches 0.6 N, the difference shrinks, so the slope shrinks. At one time constant the response reaches about 63.2% of the final change. This is a linear differential equation.

6. From a differential equation to a transfer function

6.1 What the Laplace substitution actually does

A transfer function compares an output to an input with zero initial perturbations. For that calculation, the Laplace transform turns differentiation into multiplication by ss:

L{h˙}=sH(s)−h(0)=sH(s),L{h¨}=s2H(s)−sh(0)−h˙(0)=s2H(s).\mathcal L\{\dot h\}=sH(s)-h(0)=sH(s),\qquad \mathcal L\{\ddot h\}=s^2H(s)-sh(0)-\dot h(0)=s^2H(s).

The initial conditions are not always zero in reality. We set them to zero to define the input/output transfer function; state equations can separately include a nonzero initial tilt or speed.

Transform the altitude equation term by term:

mδh¨+dzδh˙=δT⟹ms2H(s)+dzsH(s)=ΔT(s).m\delta\ddot h+d_z\delta\dot h=\delta T \quad\Longrightarrow\quad ms^2H(s)+d_zsH(s)=\Delta T(s).

Factor out H(s)H(s), then divide both sides by its coefficient and the input:

(ms2+dzs)H(s)=ΔT(s),H(s)ΔT(s)=1s(ms+dz).(ms^2+d_zs)H(s)=\Delta T(s), \qquad \boxed{\frac{H(s)}{\Delta T(s)}=\frac{1}{s(ms+d_z)}.}

No controller is involved yet. This is the plant's height response to additional total thrust.

The centered left vertical stick requests zero vertical speed. It freezes the height reference. A displaced stick requests a vertical speed, which advances that reference. The altitude PID's derivative term uses commanded minus estimated vertical speed; there is no separate seventh vertical-rate PID.

For one controller, define error e=yref−ye=y_{\rm ref}-y. The three contributions are

uP=Kpe,uI=Ki∫e dt,uD=Kde˙.u_P=K_pe,\qquad u_I=K_i\int e\,dt,\qquad u_D=K_d\dot e.

P responds to the current error. I accumulates persistent error. D responds to change and can provide damping. P00−=P00+Δt(P01+P10)+Δt2P11+σa2Δt4/4,P01−=P01+ΔtP11+σa2Δt3/2,P10−=P10+ΔtP11+σa2Δt3/2,P11−=P11+σa2Δt2.\begin{aligned} P_{00}^-&=P_{00}+\Delta t(P_{01}+P_{10})+\Delta t^2P_{11}+\sigma_a^2\Delta t^4/4,\\ P_{01}^-&=P_{01}+\Delta tP_{11}+\sigma_a^2\Delta t^3/2,\\ P_{10}^-&=P_{10}+\Delta tP_{11}+\sigma_a^2\Delta t^3/2,\\ P_{11}^-&=P_{11}+\sigma_a^2\Delta t^2. \end{aligned}

For a new height observation zhz_h, use

S=P00−+Rh,Kh=P00−/S,Kv=P10−/S,ν=zh−h^−,S=P_{00}^-+R_h,\quad K_h=P_{00}^-/S,\quad K_v=P_{10}^-/S,\quad \nu=z_h-\hat h^-,
h^+=h^−+Khν,v^+=v^−+Kvν.\hat h^+=\hat h^-+K_h\nu,\qquad \hat v^+=\hat v^-+K_v\nu.

The cross-covariance P10P_{10} lets a height measurement also correct velocity. With initial diagonal variances 1 and 1, σa=1.5\sigma_a=1.5 m/s², Rh=2R_h=2 m², and a single 2 ms prediction, P00−=1.000004000009P_{00}^-=1.000004000009, P10−=0.002000009P_{10}^-=0.002000009, giving Kh≈0.333334222K_h\approx0.333334222 and Kv≈0.000666669K_v\approx0.000666669 s⁻¹. This is an illustrative single update, not the steady-state gain or the gain after a whole barometer interval.

The filter predicts at the control rate but corrects only when a new pressure sequence arrives. Reusing one barometer reading as if it were 10 independent readings would artificially shrink covariance. Current pressure polling is about 50 Hz and the BMP filter adds correlation/delay that still needs measurement.

12.5 Preserve sensible covariance numerically

The C code uses the Joseph correction form

P+=(I−KH)P−(I−KH)T+KRKT.P^+=(I-KH)P^-(I-KH)^T+KRK^T.

For H=[1,0]H=[1,0], write P−=[[a,b],[b,d]]P^-=[[a,b],[b,d]], K=[k0,k1]TK=[k_0,k_1]^T and z=1−k0z=1-k_0. Its entries are

P00+=z2a+k02R,P01+=P10+=z(b−k1a)+k0k1R,P11+=d−2k1b+k12a+k12R.\begin{aligned} P_{00}^+&=z^2a+k_0^2R,\\ P_{01}^+=P_{10}^+&=z(b-k_1a)+k_0k_1R,\\ P_{11}^+&=d-2k_1b+k_1^2a+k_1^2R. \end{aligned}

This is the exact scalar-observation form expanded in covariance_correct(). Standard deviations are square roots of the diagonal entries. They quantify uncertainty under the assumed model; unmodeled vibration, drift or wrong axes can make the real error larger.

16. Why centring the stick requires a braking loop

Angle control does not directly command position. In the small-angle hover model from §4,

x¨≈gθ,y¨≈−gϕ.\ddot x\approx g\theta,\qquad \ddot y\approx-g\phi.

If θ=0\theta=0, then x¨≈0\ddot x\approx0. Integrating once gives x˙(t)≈x˙(0)\dot x(t)\approx\dot x(0), not necessarily zero. A level drone can keep moving. Drag reduces this eventually, but cannot promise that it stops where the stick was released.

16.1 Define the desired point and signs

At the instant of stick release, save the observed position xh=x(t0)x_h=x(t_0). Define

ex=xh−x,e˙x=−x˙=−vx,e¨x=−x¨=−ax.e_x=x_h-x,\qquad \dot e_x=-\dot x=-v_x,\qquad\ddot e_x=-\ddot x=-a_x.

There is a minus sign because the held target is constant: dxh/dt=0d x_h/dt=0. If the aircraft is moving forward, vx>0v_x>0 and e˙x<0\dot e_x<0. It is leaving the target even though the initial position error is zero.

Ask for acceleration using a position term and a braking term:

ax∗=kxex−kvvx=0.9ex−1.9vx.a_x^*=k_x e_x-k_vv_x=0.9e_x-1.9v_x.

For ex=0e_x=0 and vx>0v_x>0, this gives negative acceleration. That is the counter-tilt you see after releasing the stick. The units are kx:s−2k_x:\mathrm{s^{-2}} and kv:s−1k_v:\mathrm{s^{-1}}, so each term has acceleration units.

16.2 Derive the ideal error dynamics, one substitution at a time

Suppose the inner loops track the requested acceleration exactly, with no limit or delay. Substitute ax=ax∗a_x=a_x^* and vx=−e˙xv_x=-\dot e_x:

e¨x=−ax∗=−0.9ex+1.9vx=−0.9ex−1.9e˙x.\ddot e_x=-a_x^*=-0.9e_x+1.9v_x =-0.9e_x-1.9\dot e_x.

Bring everything to the left:

e¨x+1.9e˙x+0.9ex=0.\ddot e_x+1.9\dot e_x+0.9e_x=0.

Its characteristic polynomial factors directly:

s2+1.9s+0.9=(s+0.9)(s+1),s1=−0.9,s2=−1.s^2+1.9s+0.9=(s+0.9)(s+1),\qquad s_1=-0.9,\quad s_2=-1.

Both ideal modes decay. This is a calculation for an ideal outer loop, not a proof that the full uncertain aircraft is stable. The six inner angle/rate/height controllers, motor lag, observations and limits still matter.

16.3 Why even this stable loop overshoots the release point

Let t=0t=0 mean release, and let v0>0v_0>0 be its speed then. We have ex(0)=0e_x(0)=0 and e˙x(0)=−v0\dot e_x(0)=-v_0. The general ideal solution is

ex(t)=C1e−0.9t+C2e−t.e_x(t)=C_1e^{-0.9t}+C_2e^{-t}.

The first initial condition gives C2=−C1C_2=-C_1. Differentiating and substituting the second gives

−0.9C1−C2=0.1C1=−v0,C1=−10v0,C2=10v0.-0.9C_1-C_2=0.1C_1=-v_0, \qquad C_1=-10v_0,\quad C_2=10v_0.

Therefore

ex(t)=10v0(e−t−e−0.9t).e_x(t)=10v_0(e^{-t}-e^{-0.9t}).

For positive tt this is initially negative: the drone first moves past the release point, then returns. Our real implementation also limits acceleration to ±1.2 m/s2\pm1.2,\mathrm{m/s^2}. Even perfect immediate braking cannot stop in less than

dmin⁡=v022amax⁡.d_{\min}=\frac{v_0^2}{2a_{\max}}.

At v0=0.74 m/sv_0=0.74,\mathrm{m/s}, this lower bound is about 0.2280.228 m. At 2.67 m/s2.67,\mathrm{m/s} it is about 2.972.97 m. Finite motor/attitude response can increase it. A 3 cm error after settling is not a 3 cm maximum excursion.

16.4 Match the explanation to the 2 ms C implementation

The implemented acceleration requests are clipped:

ax=clip⁡(0.9(xh−x)−1.9vx,−1.2,1.2),ay=clip⁡(0.9(yh−y)−1.9vy,−1.2,1.2).a_x=\operatorname{clip}(0.9(x_h-x)-1.9v_x,-1.2,1.2),\quad a_y=\operatorname{clip}(0.9(y_h-y)-1.9v_y,-1.2,1.2).

For heading ψ\psi, turn world requests into body forward/left requests:

abx=cos⁡ψ ax+sin⁡ψ ay,aby=−sin⁡ψ ax+cos⁡ψ ay.a_{bx}=\cos\psi\,a_x+\sin\psi\,a_y,\qquad a_{by}=-\sin\psi\,a_x+\cos\psi\,a_y.

The LTI small-angle explanation uses θr≈abx/g\theta_r\approx a_{bx}/g, ϕr≈−aby/g\phi_r\approx-a_{by}/g. The C implementation uses bounded arctangent equivalents and converts radians to degrees before applying its ±7∘\pm7^\circ braking limit. A deflected stick still commands angle directly. These two optional outer PD corrections do not replace the six PIDs derived earlier.

Fresh horizontal localization is mandatory for this loop. The simulator explicitly supplies ideal position, velocity and heading to the C FC; Python does not override the aircraft's position or motor forces. No equivalent real localization driver is implemented. After 100 ms without fresh aiding, the FC stops using position hold. The IMU/barometer alone cannot provide drift-free XY position.

17. Gentle inputs, RT/LT and the PID headroom calculation

17.1 The stick curve does not change a PID gain

Let qq be a raw normalized stick value. After the 12% deadband, define

z=sign⁡(q)max⁡(0,∣q∣−0.120.88).z=\operatorname{sign}(q)\max\left(0,\frac{|q|-0.12}{0.88}\right).

Gentle maps roll/pitch to 5z∣z∣5z|z| degrees. Thus halfway through the active travel, z=0.5z=0.5 gives 5(0.5)2=1.25∘5(0.5)^2=1.25^\circ. Full travel gives 5∘5^\circ. The PID compares this requested angle with its estimate as before; neither KpK_p nor KiK_i is secretly multiplied by the stick curve.

Gentle is now the physical C boot default and the UI default. The simulator's explicitly labelled input mapper applies this curve once and its C host decoder is configured to avoid applying it again. Triggers retain full travel.

17.2 Trigger slew and collective ceiling

Let b∗=clip⁡(RT−LT,−1,1)b^*=\operatorname{clip}(RT-LT,-1,1). At each sample the FC updates

bk=bk−1+clip⁡(b∗−bk−1,−10Δt,4Δt),cmax⁡=0.65+0.15bk.b_k=b_{k-1}+\operatorname{clip}(b^*-b_{k-1},-10\Delta t,4\Delta t), \qquad c_{\max}=0.65+0.15b_k.

Neutral means cmax⁡=0.65c_{\max}=0.65, full RT permits 0.800.80, and full LT reduces it to 0.500.50. These are ceilings; they do not force the aircraft to use those values. Under a provisional 1000–2000 microsecond mapping,

tequiv=(1000+1000c) μs,c=0.8⇒tequiv=1800 μs.t_{\mathrm{equiv}}=(1000+1000c)\,\mathrm{\mu s}, \qquad c=0.8\Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{equiv}}=1800\,\mathrm{\mu s}.

There is no physical ESC pulse driver. This conversion does not identify thrust or guarantee how a real ESC responds.

17.3 Keep torque corrections while respecting the ceiling

Call the four zero-sum attitude corrections δi\delta_i. Let ℓ=min⁡iδi\ell=\min_i\delta_i and u=max⁡iδiu=\max_i\delta_i. Scale infeasible differential commands together, using scale α≤1\alpha\le1. The feasible collective interval must satisfy

0≤c+αδi≤1⟹−αℓ≤c≤1−αu.0\le c+\alpha\delta_i\le1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad -\alpha\ell\le c\le1-\alpha u.

Also require c≤cmax⁡c\le c_{\max}. The implemented allocation is therefore

calloc=clip⁡(crequest,−αℓ,min⁡(cmax⁡,1−αu)),mi=calloc+αδi.c_{\mathrm{alloc}}=\operatorname{clip}\left(c_{\mathrm{request}}, -\alpha\ell,\min(c_{\max},1-\alpha u)\right), \qquad m_i=c_{\mathrm{alloc}}+\alpha\delta_i.

The scale is reduced if necessary so this interval exists, including α≤cmax⁡/(−ℓ)\alpha\le c_{\max}/(-\ell) when ℓ<0\ell<0. Since ∑iδi=0\sum_i\delta_i=0,

m1+m2+m3+m44=calloc≤cmax⁡.\frac{m_1+m_2+m_3+m_4}{4}=c_{\mathrm{alloc}}\le c_{\max}.

An individual motor can exceed 0.80.8 to correct attitude while another decreases. That is the reserved PID authority, not an RT collective boost to 1.01.0. Altitude integral growth is undone when it would push further into the actual collective ceiling; integration that unwinds saturation is allowed.

18. What the present filters and experiments establish

An accelerometer observes gravity mixed with translational acceleration. A sustained tilt accelerates the aircraft, so treating every accelerometer angle as gravity can bias the attitude estimate and cause angle creep. This was observed in a preserved failed simulation.

In manual mode with fresh localization, the C core estimates horizontal acceleration from velocity differences. Above 0.25 m/s20.25,\mathrm{m/s^2} it keeps gyro prediction and covariance propagation, but skips that contaminated gravity correction:

x^k=x^k−,Pk=Pk−,Kk=0.\hat x_k=\hat x_k^-,\qquad P_k=P_k^-,\qquad K_k=0.

The filter has not become magically certain. Its prediction uncertainty continues to evolve. The correction resumes as translation settles. This depends on ideal simulated aiding today; it is not drift-free IMU-only estimation or a hardware validation. The ROS2-navigation estimator policy remains separate.
